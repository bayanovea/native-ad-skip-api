Segments: today and once AI-first is on

A conceptual picture for a frontend developer and an assessor. No internals. Two phases, one shared principle: viewers get only the segments we are sure of, even if that means missing some ads.

What is true and what is served: two steps

Every segment the AI finds is kept. Whether it may reach the viewer is worked out in advance, in two steps:

1. Is it an ad?the AI's answer, if it is sure enough: a sponsor needs medium confidence, self-promotion high. An assessor's verdict overrides both the AI and these thresholds: "ad" makes even a doubtful segment ready to serve, "not an ad" sets it aside for good.
2. May it be served?at least 5 seconds long, for every segment, with a verdict or without.

Nothing is deleted: the rest is set aside with its reason, "not sure", "too short", "the second model did not find it" or "an assessor said no". When the owner changes a rule (say, the minimum length), every AI markup is recomputed once; assessors' verdicts stay the last word.

Second opinion: two models must agree

On since 2026-11-01 (a switch of its own; off from 2026-11-07 to 11-19, when the money for it ran short). A second model from another vendor reads the same subtitle text on its own: OpenAI's model finds the ads, Anthropic's (Claude) checks them (the owner's choice; a model is changed only after it has been tested on reviewed examples). A segment that passed both steps above is ready to serve only when the second model found an ad of the same category in about the same place.

Both found itthe segment is ready to serve, with the first model's edges.
Only the first found itthe segment is set aside ("the second model did not find it") and goes to an assessor, at most 30 of them a day (the rest simply stay set aside); an "ad" verdict puts it on the air, "not an ad" sets it aside for good.

What only the second model found is never served and never sent to an assessor: we only count it. If the second model does not answer, the video is published from the first model, as without the switch.

Budget: each vendor's money for the month

A switch of its own; until it is on, the detector takes a fixed number of videos a day and the second model checks every one. The models are paid from two monthly allowances, one per vendor, each renewing on its own day; what is left at the renewal is lost. With the budget on, every few minutes the pipeline works out today's share of each: what is left of the allowance (less a small reserve), spread evenly over the days to the renewal. A quiet day leaves more for the next ones, a busy day less.

The detector (OpenAI)takes only as many new videos as today's share pays for (never more than the fixed daily number); the rest wait in the queue, most-watched first as always.
The second opinion (Anthropic)when today's share is spent, the check is postponed, never the video: it is published from the first model alone, every segment that passed the two steps above goes live, and the video waits for its check.

Postponed videos are checked later, newest first, as the following days' money allows; the videos published while the second opinion was off are among them. A video that shows nothing is not checked: a late check could not change it. To clear the queue faster, the owner can add a fixed sum to Anthropic's daily share for a while; the following days pay it back out of theirs. A late check never adds a segment: one the second model does not find is set aside ("the second model did not find it") and goes to an assessor within the same 30 a day; a segment an assessor has already confirmed or given edges stays live. The judge and the batch audit are counted in Anthropic's spend but never held back. A day that spends far past its share raises an alert.

The author's chapters: sharper edges

On since 2026-11-02 (a switch of its own). Many videos have chapters in their description, the author's timecodes such as "2:15 Ad"; every detector run saves them. Once both models agree, the segment is checked against the chapters.

A chapter matchesa chapter overlaps the segment and both its start and its end are within 10 seconds of the segment's edges → viewers get the chapter's edges (by time only, the title is not read). That is the author's timing: it also covers the parts of an ad without words, which the subtitles cannot see.
Only one edge matchesa chapter whose title looks like an ad (ad, integration, sponsor, promo code…) has one edge near the segment and the other far away (the chapter runs 16:48–25:05, the ad ends at 18:03) → viewers keep the AI's edges and the segment goes to an assessor (item T, priority Important).

A chapter never creates a segment on its own and never puts a set-aside one on the air. AI markups published before the check was switched on are not checked again.

Shifted subtitles: why the track check comes first

The detector sees only the subtitle text and its timing. If the subtitle track belongs to another version of the video (longer, cut differently), every time is shifted and a "clean" segment lands in the wrong place.

video
subtitles ok
subtitles shifted

The rule is simple: if the subtitle track is noticeably longer than the video, or the video exists in several cuts of different length and the subtitles fit none of them, the AI publishes nothing for that video. Missing an ad beats cutting the wrong thing. A separate story (later): measure the shift from the audio and move the segments back.

The assessor in phase 1

The assessor checks the AI's findings on the review page. Two independent answers per item:

  • Verdict: what the text is: sponsor self_promotion not_ad unclear. Judge the text, not the picture.
  • Flag "subtitles ≠ video": a fact about the whole video, not the segment. Set separately; it never replaces the verdict.
  • Start and end times (optional): when the AI's edges are visibly off and a viewer would notice, select the text in the subtitles around the item or type the time from the player. Only with sponsor / self_promotion. Where the edge is: the lead-in ("and now a word from our sponsor") is part of the ad, the return to the topic is not, "link in the description" is part of the ad. Shorter than 5 seconds is saved too; the page warns that viewers will not get it while that rule stands.

These verdicts tell us how far the detector can be trusted and decide when phase 2 can start.

For the frontend

  • The API and the response format do not change.
  • Send views_text with the segments request: the view counter as the page shows it (“39.4K views”); the backend reads the number out of it. A video goes to the detector when that counter is high enough (threshold set by the owner) or when enough of our users requested it (a second threshold); both thresholds are how the owner controls the spend. Among the eligible videos the detector takes the ones with the most viewers and the most likely ads first; one not reached in two weeks is skipped.

For the assessor

  • Items come in batches from sheets; the first free reviewer takes a batch, it is theirs until the last verdict.
  • Until there is a verdict, who flagged the segment stays hidden (blind review).

Users' own segments on an AI video

Decided by the owner on 2026-11-25 and 11-26, a switch of its own; on since 2026-11-26. Until then a video with an AI markup showed only the AI's segments: a viewer's new markup was saved and shown to nobody. The model reads subtitles and does not see the picture, so it is there to fill what users did not mark, not to correct what they did. Once a minute the users' segments are written into the AI markup, each marked source: "user" until an assessor has checked it. Only ads marked after the switch-on date count, by authors in good standing and at least 5 seconds long; intro and credits count whatever their age.

Nothing of the AI's there → shown at oncewith the user's edges; an assessor gets it right away (item U, priority Urgent). "Ad" makes it the assessor's segment, "not an ad" removes it for good.
A short addition → gluedthe user extended an AI segment of the same category, or marked right next to it, adding at most 20 seconds: viewers get one segment over both, no assessor. The AI's own segment waits aside and comes back if the user deletes the markup.
A larger overlap → waitsthe AI segment stays as it is; the user's stretch goes to an assessor (item U over both), whose "ad" merges them into one.
Intro and credits → shown as they areunless they overlap an AI segment; assessors have no such verdict, so no item.

A user's ad that the AI segments already cover by half or more changes nothing: the AI's edges stay. A user's segment alone never keeps an AI markup on the air: when an assessor removes the AI's last segment, the whole video goes back to the human segments. Switched off, every unchecked user segment is gone within a minute and the glued AI segments are back with their own edges.

The judge: who watches for AI mistakes

Once an hour the judge checks the published AI markups that got new negative signals: dislikes, undone skips, human markups elsewhere, a new cut of the video. Likes and agreeing markups give no reason to look. It judges the AI's segments only: votes and undos on a user's segment do not call it (a user's ad already has its assessor item), and a markup that is already shown as a user's segment is no disagreement.

The extension also reports every skip it made, every undo (the "return" button or a seek back into the segment) and a forward seek right after a skip (the ad went on). Skips give undos a denominator: the judge reads "2 undos in 40 skips". For now these signals are only collected: with the AI_JUDGE_UNDO_EVIDENCE=off switch an undo alone does not call the judge; it is recorded and shown when something else does.

The assessor in phase 2: a verdict changes what is live

Next to the usual sheets come batches from the judge (ai-judge-<date>), marked "AI segment" with the judge's reason, optional batches of the segments the AI was not sure of (ai-below-threshold-<date>), batches of the segments the second model did not find (ai-second-<date>, items D), segments an admin sent by hand (ai-admin-<date>, items M), blind samples of what viewers get now (ai-served-<date>, items S) batches of the segments where an author's chapter matches the AI on one edge only (ai-chapter-<date>, items T) and the users' own ads on AI videos (ai-user-<date>, items U, priority Urgent). The decision is the same as before, but now it acts at once:

sponsor / self_promotionthe segment stays live (or comes back, or goes live for the first time if the AI was not sure of it) with that category; when the assessor gave times, those edges go live (under 5 seconds: saved, not served); times that cover neighbouring segments of the same category merge them into one (the detector sometimes cuts one ad into pieces; the page says how many it covers). If the judge withdrew the whole markup and every segment still in it is confirmed, the markup is live again.
not_ad / unclearthe segment is taken off the AI markup and set aside for good: no rule brings it back, only a later verdict. Once all the AI's and assessors' segments are removed, the markup is withdrawn and viewers get the human segments (users' unchecked segments do not hold it).

The "subtitles ≠ video" flag is set as before, separately from the verdict. It accumulates as knowledge about the video and feeds the later work on shifted tracks.

For the frontend

  • Same format, same voting. The new source and confidence fields can be ignored or shown; source: "review" means an assessor set the edges, source: "user" a user's segment nobody has checked yet (it has no confidence).
  • Always send the video duration: without it no AI markup is served (we cannot tell which cut it is).
  • Skips, undone skips and dislikes are valuable events; they reach the judge.

For the assessor

  • AI items come in batches from the judge, one per segment; real viewers are behind them, so they come before the sheets.
  • Decide by the text, as before. One verdict puts one segment on or off the air.
  • Start and end times with a sponsor / self-promotion verdict change the segment's edges for viewers at once; votes on the old edges are not carried over. Edges under 5 seconds are saved but not served.
  • In the batches of doubtful segments a sponsor / self-promotion verdict puts the segment on the air although the AI was not sure (unless it is shorter than 5 seconds: then set the real times).
  • In the ai-second batches the first model found the segment and the second did not; it is off the air until you say sponsor / self-promotion. Some come from a late check of a video that was already live: the segment was on the air until that check.
  • In the ai-chapter batches the video's chapters are shown above the subtitles, each with a "use the chapter's times" button that fills the verdict's start and end with that chapter's times. Use it only when the chapter matches what you see in the player; otherwise type or select the right times, or answer not an ad as usual.
  • In the ai-user batches (U) the stretch is a viewer's own ad on a video the AI already marked. Some are on the air until you decide; others overlap an AI segment and the item covers both, so "ad" without times merges them (the page lists the video's other segments). Judge the text as always; set times when the viewer's edges are visibly off.
  • The track flag is a separate checkbox; it never replaces the verdict.

Safety nets

  • Kill switch. One flag turns AI-first off entirely: the extension gets the human segments again, no data is touched.
  • Per video. An admin can withdraw the AI markup of one video; verdicts never bring that one back.
  • Human markups live on in parallel. They are never lost and always ready to replace the AI.
  • Rules are changed by a recompute. The confidence thresholds and the minimum length are the owner's to change; a change recomputes every AI markup once, and verdicts stay the last word.
  • Second opinion. Its own switch: turning it off publishes new videos from one model again; segments already set aside wait for a verdict.
  • Budget. The detector handles a limited number of videos per day; with the budget switch on, also no more than each vendor's share for the day. Overspend raises an alert.
  • Users' segments. Their own switch: turning it off takes every unchecked user segment off the AI markups within a minute and puts glued AI segments back; segments an assessor confirmed stay.
