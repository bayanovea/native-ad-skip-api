What is true and what is served: two steps

Every segment the AI finds is kept. Whether it may reach the viewer is worked out in advance, in two steps:

1. Is it an ad?the AI's answer, if it is sure enough: a sponsor needs medium confidence, self-promotion high. An assessor's verdict overrides both the AI and these thresholds: "ad" makes even a doubtful segment ready to serve, "not an ad" sets it aside for good.
2. May it be served?at least 5 seconds long, for every segment, with a verdict or without.

Nothing is deleted: the rest is set aside with its reason, "not sure", "too short" or "an assessor said no". When the owner changes a rule (say, the minimum length), every AI markup is recomputed once; assessors' verdicts stay the last word.

Shifted subtitles: why the track check comes first

The detector sees only the subtitle text and its timing. If the subtitle track belongs to another version of the video (longer, cut differently), every time is shifted and a "clean" segment lands in the wrong place.

The rule is simple: if the subtitle track is noticeably longer than the video, or the video exists in several cuts of different length and the subtitles fit none of them, the AI publishes nothing for that video. Missing an ad beats cutting the wrong thing. A separate story (later): measure the shift from the audio and move the segments back.

The assessor in phase 1

The assessor checks the AI's findings on the review page. Two independent answers per item:

  • Verdict: what the text is: sponsor self_promotion not_ad unclear. Judge the text, not the picture.
  • Flag "subtitles ≠ video": a fact about the whole video, not the segment. Set separately; it never replaces the verdict.
  • Start and end times (optional): when the AI's edges are visibly off and a viewer would notice, select the text in the subtitles around the item or type the time from the player. Only with sponsor / self_promotion. Where the edge is: the lead-in ("and now a word from our sponsor") is part of the ad, the return to the topic is not, "link in the description" is part of the ad. Shorter than 5 seconds is saved too; the page warns that viewers will not get it while that rule stands.

These verdicts tell us how far the detector can be trusted and decide when phase 2 can start.

For the frontend

  • The API and the response format do not change.
  • Send views_text with the segments request: the view counter as the page shows it (“39.4K views”); the backend reads the number out of it. The AI then picks truly popular videos instead of counting requests.

For the assessor

  • Items come in batches from sheets; the first free reviewer takes a batch, it is theirs until the last verdict.
  • Until there is a verdict, who flagged the segment stays hidden (blind review).

One interval, one vote. When a human segment has the same bounds as an AI one, the viewer's vote counts for both and for the markup authors too. The viewer does not care who drew the segment.

The judge: who watches for AI mistakes

Once an hour the judge checks the published AI markups that got new negative signals: dislikes, undone skips, human markups elsewhere, a new cut of the video. Likes and agreeing markups give no reason to look.

The judge never closes a case on its own: a human has the last word. It only pulls doubtful segments and calls an assessor.

The assessor in phase 2: a verdict changes what is live

Next to the usual sheets come batches from the judge (ai-judge-<date>), marked "AI segment" with the judge's reason, and optional batches of the segments the AI was not sure of (ai-below-threshold-<date>). The decision is the same as before, but now it acts at once:

sponsor / self_promotionthe segment stays live (or comes back, or goes live for the first time if the AI was not sure of it) with that category; when the assessor gave times, those edges go live (under 5 seconds: saved, not served). If the judge withdrew the whole markup and every segment of it is confirmed, the markup is live again.
not_ad / unclearthe segment is taken off the AI markup and set aside for good: no rule brings it back, only a later verdict. Once all are removed, the markup is withdrawn and viewers get the human segments.

The "subtitles ≠ video" flag is set as before, separately from the verdict. It accumulates as knowledge about the video and feeds the later work on shifted tracks.

For the frontend

  • Same format, same voting. The new source and confidence fields can be ignored or shown; source: "review" means an assessor set the edges.
  • Always send the video duration: without it no AI markup is served (we cannot tell which cut it is).
  • Undone skips and dislikes are valuable events; they reach the judge.

For the assessor

  • AI items come in batches from the judge, one per segment; real viewers are behind them, so they come before the sheets.
  • Decide by the text, as before. One verdict puts one segment on or off the air.
  • Start and end times with a sponsor / self-promotion verdict change the segment's edges for viewers at once; votes on the old edges are not carried over. Edges under 5 seconds are saved but not served.
  • In the batches of doubtful segments a sponsor / self-promotion verdict puts the segment on the air although the AI was not sure.
  • The track flag is a separate checkbox; it never replaces the verdict.

Safety nets

  • Kill switch. One flag turns AI-first off entirely: the extension gets the human segments again, no data is touched.
  • Per video. An admin can withdraw the AI markup of one video; verdicts never bring that one back.
  • Human markups live on in parallel. They are never lost and always ready to replace the AI.
  • Rules are changed by a recompute. The confidence thresholds and the minimum length are the owner's to change; a change recomputes every AI markup once, and verdicts stay the last word.
  • Budget. The detector handles a limited number of videos per day; overspend raises an alert.
